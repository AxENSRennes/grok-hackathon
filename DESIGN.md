# Visual direction

This brief records the user's choices, not a new design proposal.

- Product: a shared pixel-art game for animating a hackathon. The fun comes from people moving, jumping, pushing each other, and answering questions by position.
- Shared screen: start with a large centered QR code and a single Play button. Players can join the room before launch. Play reveals the arena on the same page and keeps the same QR code small in the corner. Minimal text. No dashboard or visible round-management interface.
- Arrival: scan QR, enter a nickname, choose a character and one or two accessories, appear in the room. No account creation. Character selection is a video-game carousel: large animated preview in the center, neighboring characters visible, swipe or left/right controls to browse; accessories are selected visually and previewed on the character. No text lists or dropdowns.
- Characters: small, simple, rounded/blocky silhouettes between old-school Pokémon and Among Us, slightly more human. Easy to distinguish in a crowd. The generated character sheets are references, not approved production sprites.
- Approved art direction: the arcade family in `design/character-palettes-2026-10-22/06-arcade-community-night.png`. Cyan, hot pink, yellow, orange and violet character clothing, dark navy pixel outlines, compact human chibi silhouettes, distinct hair and varied skin tones. All screens should belong to this same arcade world.
- Approved first-character visual: `design/arcade-character-01/cyan-hoodie-poses-v1.png`, explicitly liked by the user. Use its cyan hoodie, dark hair, compact proportions and navy outlines as the starting template. Visual approval does not mean its pose grid or animation has already been normalized or integrated.
- Selected large-screen environment: proposal 01, the arcade hall, with a larger question. The coded interpretation uses a big pixel-letter question board, cyan/pink answer areas, a small robot presenter and decorative cabinets around a quiet playable floor. `/preview` shows two local code-drawn characters for visual review, separate from live rooms.
- Environment: readable 2D pixel art, coherent with the approved arcade characters. Dark ink-blue/navy bases keep bright avatars legible; cyan and pink can distinguish answer zones, with restrained yellow accents. Details stay at the edges so the playable floor stays clear. The pixel-art language also applies to the phone page. The precise environment is being explored in five large-screen mockups.
- Phone: a remote only. Upper half has four directional controls around a central jump button; lower half has the text-entry area. No map on the phone.
- Interaction: jumps without an overhead locator arrow; balanced pushing; short-lived speech bubbles. Bubbles may be removed if they interfere with question responses.
- Questions: answers occupy spatial zones. Final position counts. The map/zones can change; their final presentation is still open.
- Host: a pixelated Grok presenter. The split between Grok and organizer controls is still undecided.
- Identity and motion: repeated pixel edges and compact characters provide identity; movement, jumps, and collisions provide the visual energy. Interface decoration and text stay sparse.

The arcade family and palette are selected. The environment, final animated sprite assets, and final typography remain open. Existing placeholder art in the application is not a finalized visual decision.
